# Paid income routing: pool in Cart or split toward One-click?

## Result and boundary

There is a small ordinary routing decision on one genuinely acquired board: changing **only one Mail link's `routeTo`** changes whether two $2 income feeds pool in Cart or accumulate separately in Cart and a genuinely fused One-click. Cash, owned items, geometry, CPU, administrators, income generation and natural starting clocks are identical between the two choices.

At the acquired board's round-six base HP380 and purchased CPU21, pooling wins against the existing native fortress while splitting loses; splitting wins against twelve ordinary links while pooling loses. **The fortress costs $74 and has CPU32 load, so it is overloaded in that CPU21 comparison. At hypothetical common CPU32, it defeats both choices.** This is a conditional routing-only decision inside a stated capacity/timing slice, not a new fortress counter or broad archetype diversity. Both choices also lose to a cheaper $23 supported-four-link board.

The two narrow wins fail a hypothetical 0.75-second subject startup delay. No balance, shop, recipe, opponent, story or gameplay clock was changed. This is distinct from the earlier five-move story commerce recovery and from the Bandcamp-inspired template movement example.

## Why the destination matters

Both Mail links naturally produce $2 at 2.65, 7.85, 13.05 and 18.30 seconds in the canonical witness. Cart spends each $3 for fixed15 damage; One-click spends each $3 for fixed20. The existing natural One-click attacks continue in both branches, and use the same cumulative income. Routing does not alter the producer cadence or duplicate income.

| Through time | Total income | Pool: conversion damage / remaining charge | Split: conversion damage / remaining charge |
| ------------ | -----------: | -----------------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------: |
| 2.65s        |            4 |                                     15 / 1 |                                       0 / 4 |
| 7.85s        |            8 |                                     30 / 2 |                                      35 / 2 |
| 13.05s       |           12 |                                     60 / 0 |                                      70 / 0 |
| 18.30s       |           16 |                                     75 / 1 |                                      70 / 4 |

These are pre-mitigation conversion payloads, separate from natural attacks. Pooling crosses a shared $3 threshold sooner, while splitting uses a stronger recipient when its own threshold is met. Unspent per-recipient residue means the finite-time advantage can alternate; this is not a universal “early Cart, late One-click” rule. The report records the real route/spend events and asserts identical income events over every matched pair's common observation window.

### Startup sensitivity

The engine's production startup is unchanged. Stress adds 0.75 seconds once to every timed component of the logical subject or opponent separately, preserving later periods and all stats. These hypothetical delays are not editor options. At HP380/CPU21:

- Delay the subject: its pooled fortress win becomes a loss, with20 enemy HP; its split twelve-link win becomes a draw
- Delay the fortress: the previously losing split route also wins, with13.28 subject HP
- At adequately provisioned CPU32, fortress defeats both routes under all three starts

The report records each canonical and stressed starting clock. Both seats agree on the complete logical result, per-part accounting and normalized subject income/conversion events in every measured case. This narrowly establishes an accessible destination choice with real acquisition and clear failure conditions. It does not justify tuning or certify general balance.
